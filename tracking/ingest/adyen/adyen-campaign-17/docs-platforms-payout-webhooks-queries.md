Q1. What is this document's purpose and scope, and what must not be inferred?
A1. It is a 2026-09-15 Adyen for Platforms guide to Transfer webhooks for an outgoing bank payout from a user's balance account, using `balancePlatform.transfer.created` for request creation and `balancePlatform.transfer.updated` for state changes (raw lines 15–24, 62–71). Its payout-related selector is `category=bank`, `direction=outgoing`, `type=bankTransfer` (raw lines 38–46). It is distinct from the legacy Online payouts/Payout API webhook route. A created, authorised, booked, estimated-arrival or tracing event does not by itself prove beneficiary-bank credit; test payloads and placeholder identifiers are not account-specific proof (raw lines 62–142, 327–336, 832–838, 952–976). Q2. What is the central capability, what prerequisites and consequential warnings apply?
A2. The page requires a payout-service integration, a webhook-capable server and Customer Area subscriptions to Transfer and Transaction webhooks (raw lines 26–36). `created` means an outgoing request was initiated; `authorised` reserves balance-account funds, while insufficient balance gives `refused/notEnoughBalance` and requires a new transfer request after funding (raw lines 62–71, 140–142, 225–232). `booked` records deduction from the user's balance account and a transaction ID, but is explicitly nonfinal; later internal review may be pending or fail, instant bank rejection can fail after retries, and a regular/wire transfer may be returned (raw lines 327–336, 436–445, 575–583, 708–716, 1229–1235). Local-payout `estimatedArrivalTime` is only an estimate; tracing identifiers help investigate network progress; the instant `tracking.status=credited` event is the guide's stronger credit confirmation, though a sample is not actual bank receipt proof (raw lines 832–838, 952–976, 1113–1115). Permanent return reasons can block a bank transfer instrument and cause new requests to fail with HTTP 422; inspect reason/networkReason before deciding on any retry (raw lines 1351–1371). Distinguish transfer ID, event ID, accounting transaction ID, merchant references and network tracing IDs; inconsistent and reused sample values do not form a reliable join (raw lines 64–71, 99–136, 585–706, 952–1111).
