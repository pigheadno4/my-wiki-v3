Q1. How does Adyen for Platforms make processed funds available through sales-day settlement?
A. This 2026-09-15 source says processed transaction funds pass through a merchant account, are split between user and platform balance accounts, and are settled in batches assigned to the capture sales day. The default local sales day runs from midnight to the following 23:59, with configurable closing time; batches settle after the configured delay, independently of scheme/payment-method remittance timing. Net settlement subtracts costs, refunds, chargebacks, reversal and adjustments, some related to other sales days. Settlement makes balance funds available and does not show payout or beneficiary bank receipt. Raw locators: lines 15–19, 30–56; source `## Overview` and `## Key takeaways`. Q2. What timing, payout-product, and reversal qualifications matter?
A. Contract defaults for settlement delay vary by region and payment method. Managed payouts optimize delay to the contract ETA; custom payouts can configure a business-day delay before batch funds become available for scheduled/on-demand requests. Working-day and bank-holiday rules affect timing. Pass-through settlement is deprecated for new integrations except stated Affirm and `maestro_USA` variants, which follow method schedules. If Adyen has not received captured-payment funds within 30 days, a reversed settlement subtracts them from the current batch; this is not a shopper refund.
