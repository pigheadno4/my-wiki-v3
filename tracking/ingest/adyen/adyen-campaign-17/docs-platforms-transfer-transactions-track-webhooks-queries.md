Q1. What is this document's purpose and scope, and what must not be inferred?
A1. It is a 2026-09-15 guide to `balancePlatform.transaction.created` webhooks for balance-account debits and credits arising from external or internal transfers, with outgoing and incoming examples (raw lines 15–21, 33–45, 209–214). The requirements list Adyen for Platforms, Issuing or Payouts integrations (raw lines 23–31). Transfer webhooks cover transfer status changes; Transaction webhooks fire when funds are credited or debited at a balance account, so a booked transaction event is not a beneficiary-bank receipt, final payout, card outcome or proof of legal fund ownership (raw lines 19–21, 75–83, 216–226). Q2. What is the central capability, what prerequisites and consequential warnings apply?
A2. Subscribe to Standard and Transaction webhooks and have a server that accepts them; Transfer webhooks are listed as optional status context (raw lines 23–31). Outgoing external and internal examples show a negative booked transaction amount from the source balance account after the transfer is booked, while incoming external and internal examples show a positive booked amount at the target balance account (raw lines 47–127, 129–207, 216–327). The transaction `data.id` is its own identifier and `data.transfer.id` identifies the related transfer; examples reuse transaction IDs across different related transfers, so they are not a canonical join (raw lines 82–83, 91–106, 173–186, 234–253, 292–306). The incoming-external prose says third-party transfer, but the sample has `transfer.categoryData.type=platformPayment` and PSP payment references; do not infer a universal third-party bank schema from that sample (raw lines 216–253). This page does not cover bank-rejection, return or final receipt states; follow the payout Transfer webhook source where those outcomes matter.
