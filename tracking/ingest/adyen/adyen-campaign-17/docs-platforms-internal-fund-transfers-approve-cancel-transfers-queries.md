# Fixed retrieval checks — Platforms transfer approval and cancellation

Q1. What does this source govern, and what must not be inferred from its approval or examples?
- Expected: A 2020-modified Adyen for Platforms Transfers API guide captured 2026-09-15 for optional additional review of an initiated transfer. A `review` object in POST `/transfers` makes the request pending; a permitted team reviewer approves or cancels before Adyen processes it. Approval authorizes continued processing, cancellation fails the pending request, and no action for 30 days fails it. This page is filed under internal fund transfers, yet its sample webhook payloads use `category: bank` and `type: bankTransfer`; they do not prove a two-sided internal balance-account booking. An HTTP 200 approval response, `authorised` status, or test sample does not prove later booking, external bank receipt, legal fund ownership or live eligibility.

Q2. Which reviewer permission, batch limit, expiry and webhook states distinguish the decisions?
- Expected: A web service API key needs `TransferService Approval role`, and Transfer Webhooks must be subscribed. Approve and cancel use distinct POST `/transfers/approve` and `/transfers/cancel` endpoints with one to 500 `transferIds`; examples target test endpoints and library requests show idempotency keys. A transfer-created webhook with `status: received`, `reason: pending`, and `review.numberOfApprovalsRequired: 1` identifies the pending-review example. An approved transfer-updated webhook uses `status: authorised`, `reason: approved`; a cancelled example uses `status: cancelled`, `reason: unknown`. The source says no reviewer action within 30 days makes the request fail, but does not give an expiry payload or a retry, reversal, post-processing cancellation, or receipt rule. The bank-transfer sample's balance fields must not be converted into an internal-transfer or beneficiary receipt claim.
