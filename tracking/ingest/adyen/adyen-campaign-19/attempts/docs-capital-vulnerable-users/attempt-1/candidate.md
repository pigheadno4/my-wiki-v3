## Overview

This 2026-09-15 [[adyen]] Capital instruction page provides UK- and Ireland-scoped guidance for platform staff to identify and assist users who may be in vulnerable circumstances. It expressly says it is not legal advice. See [[adyen-capital]] for the product route and [[adyen-financial-products]] for broader compliance context. It requires staff distribution and understanding, describes consent-sensitive support and factual recordkeeping, and does not establish that a platform has fulfilled its duties or that any particular user has a legal or medical status.

## Key takeaways

- The page is labelled **UK and Ireland only**. It says platforms are required to provide these instructions to all staff who directly interact with users, including support, direct-contact sales/marketing and their managers, and must ensure those staff read and fully understand them. Staff should have time to support a distressed user and be able to seek clarification from Adyen. These are instructions, not proof of staff training or legal compliance. [Raw: lines 15–39]
- Adyen defines vulnerability by heightened susceptibility to harm arising from personal circumstances and uses four drivers: health, life events, resilience and capability. The B2B examples especially mention sole proprietors and small businesses, but they are indicative; vulnerability may be temporary or change over time. Do not diagnose a user or infer vulnerability solely from a business category, language or single cue. [Raw: lines 41–69, 77–114]
- The staff process begins with empathetic interaction and recognition of potential indicators. The `BRUCE` questions are a decision-making aid; `TEXAS` covers thanking, explaining use of information, **explicit consent to note and save disclosed information**, asking about impact and signposting/referral. `IDEA` helps ask about impact, duration, experience and assistance when more detail is needed. These are support methods, not authority to collect or disclose sensitive information without an appropriate basis. [Raw: lines 71–145]
- Support should be tailored through plain language, preferred channel checks, feedback, extra time or referral/signposting when needed. Listed external categories are non-exhaustive and need a relevant local organization. Worked examples of a staff member checking whether payment time can be extended are **not** a promise of a fee extension, grant restructuring or repayment relief. [Raw: lines 147–169, 184–192]
- The page states a general principle that users should share vulnerability information only once, then staff must attach factual indicators/support/follow-up notes to the customer file for appropriate future assistance. It warns against invented diagnoses or naming a condition unless the user expressly disclosed it, shows unacceptable speculative notes, and directs consultation with internal privacy/legal teams on local privacy obligations such as GDPR. Follow-up may be scheduled if needed. Recording duties must be read alongside the explicit-consent step; neither recordkeeping nor a support script proves compliant handling. [Raw: lines 126–134, 171–204]
