Q1. What does Classic Score do, and what is needed to use this overview's feature?

This 2020-modified Classic Adyen for Platforms overview describes risk signals drawn from account-holder verification data and transactions, a 0–100 unusual-activity score, and linked-account “identities” (pinned raw lines 19–34, 50–60). It says to contact an Adyen Account Manager to enable Score (21). Cases and scores are viewed in Customer Area at **Platform > Score** or the account holder's **Score** tab (45–48); detailed action and notification setup belong to linked child guides (85–95). The page redirects new integrations to the newer model, so do not map these Classic terms directly to modern Platforms resources (19). Q2. What does a high score mean, and who acts on it?

A score of 100 is the highest unusualness score but **does not confirm fraud**; the platform must review for true malicious behavior versus false positives (raw 50–56). Linked holders share signals and the same identity score, with the 20-to-100 story only an example (58–69). Adyen **does not disable payouts or processing by default**; the platform reviews and chooses manual or its own automated action, with possible disable/suspend/false-positive/refund actions listed (36–43). KYC signals are described as real-time, transaction signals daily, and platform-defined custom/high-risk-list signals real-time (71–83). These are snapshot signal timings, not proof that any specific payout was blocked, payment refunded or account sanctioned.
