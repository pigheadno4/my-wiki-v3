# Open banking query probes

## Q1 — Who is this Business Accounts open-banking overview for, what can each TPP role seek, and what must precede access?

The captured 2026-09-15 overview addresses third-party providers (TPPs), not a platform opening its own account. It names AISP for Business Account information, PISP for initiating payments from those accounts, and PIISP for available-funds information; Adyen services the accounts (raw lines 21–27, 43–58). A TPP seeking access must be nationally licensed, hold a QWAC and complete Adyen onboarding; the page asks for a PEM certificate, redirect URL, website and requested role scopes before Adyen reviews information and supplies OAuth client credentials (raw lines 31–39, 94–112). The holder's agreed scopes, authentication and consent govern API use (raw lines 79–92). The page's opening description of PSD2 as introduced in 2018 is historical framing, not fresh legal advice (raw line 19). ## Q2 — Do credentials or the UK check-mark row prove current UK support, holder consent, a payment, or an external person's bank-account verification?

No. This page twice calls its documentation EU-only and points UK readers to separate UK documentation (raw lines 36, 114–116), yet it also names a UK API, `obuk` path and UK availability row (lines 57–73, 118–121). That is an internal documentation conflict, not current UK eligibility proof. Client ID/secret follow Adyen's TPP onboarding review, while the holder must separately authenticate and consent to agreed scopes before API use (lines 83–112). None of these steps proves payment initiation, funds availability or settlement. The distinct [[source-adyen-docs-business-accounts-verify-account-holder]] concerns an **unregistered external individual's** bank-account ownership check through an AISP; its source overview and raw route distinguish that verification actor from this TPP-to-Adyen-Business-Account interface. This page also expressly separates its interface from Checkout Pay by Bank (raw line 29).
