# Retrieval checks — FX fee disclosure

## Q1 — purpose and prerequisites

**Question:** What must the user see when a transaction converts currencies, and what is this page's scope?

**Located answer:** The page says a user must be shown the FX rate and additional fees at the transaction point and in relevant-period statements for any currency conversion (raw line 21). The sample layouts expose the conversion rate, conversion value and paid fee (raw lines 25-72). Although the page sits under Business Accounts, its introduction calls it financial-products/Platforms guidance and the closing sentence says UK FCA-based presentation guidance applies to platforms in all regions (raw lines 19 and 101). It disclaims legal advice (raw line 19).

## Q2 — material boundary or conflict

**Question:** Do the fixed-fee and third-party-fee examples establish a reliable rate or guaranteed bank receipt?

**Located answer:** No. The first fixed-fee table says `Fixed fee £0.00`, `Total fees added £3.00`, a 1.26 USD-per-GBP rate and `They receive £126.10`, an inconsistent fee/currency presentation (raw lines 38-48). The variable and later fixed-fee amounts are examples, including a 3% markup sentence, not a universal current tariff (raw lines 25-72). In a separate third-party example, the page describes `Pay all fees?` as a flat upfront fee guaranteeing the intended recipient amount; the next example says third-party fees are deducted from the recipient's amount (raw lines 74-99). That illustrated option is not evidence that a live payment reached a bank.
