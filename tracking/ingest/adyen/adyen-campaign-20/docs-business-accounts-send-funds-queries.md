## Q1 — How does a Business Accounts user send funds to a third-party bank account, and what inputs and routing constraints does this page identify?

The page describes a business-related outbound transfer from the user's Adyen business account to a third-party-owned bank account, which is not the user's legal-entity transfer instrument (raw lines 19–21). It says the user's business account is debited and outgoing bank-transfer webhooks inform the platform server (line 21). The request needs `accountIdentification.type`, selected from recipient bank location and currency, with type-specific details (lines 147–158); destination tables locate supported currency/priority, description limits and bank-account formats (lines 164–205). Account-region USD/EUR/GBP priority matrices are at lines 45–76. ## Q2 — Can the guide's cut-off examples or `crossBorder` label guarantee same-day bank receipt or determine a live request priority?

No. The 2022-modified snapshot says before-cut-off transfers are *typically processed* within the settlement delay, and after-cut-off transfers move to the next day (raw lines 78–80). Its SEPA wire example says 16:30 CET, while the table lists 16:55 CET (lines 90–91, 129); its US example has conflicting next-day/same-day framing (lines 93–98). Cross-border priority can incur intermediary-bank delays and fees that reduce the beneficiary's received amount (lines 37–39). The guide also says cross-border transfers must be sent as wire transfers although it labels a separate `crossBorder` priority (lines 37, 158, 199–205). These conflicts and the dated tables preclude a live timing or priority guarantee; bank receipt requires separate transaction evidence.
