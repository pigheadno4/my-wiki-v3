# C08 POS loyalty payment-data — fixed retrieval checks

1. **Q: Which identifiers can recognize a shopper in this Terminal API loyalty guide, and can an alias or LoyaltyID itself be used as a payment credential?**
   **A:** The `alias` represents a payment card's PAN for recognition, appears in `AdditionalResponse`, and can also appear as `PaymentToken.TokenValue` when `TokenRequestedType=Customer`; Adyen explicitly says the alias **cannot be used to make payments**. For an NFC wallet, the guide says there is no PAN/card alias and directs matching by `PaymentAccountReference`. A merchant-created `shopperReference` identifies its customer profile, must be case-sensitive, at least three characters and omit PII such as name/email; `shopperEmail` is optional. The merchant must obtain consent to save the profile and send those identifiers. Return of reference/email requires `recurringProcessingModel=CardOnFile` in the initial payment, which triggers terminal PIN authentication unless Support changes that behavior for no-PIN cards; the guide warns against setting it for every transaction. The Estonia-only national-ID card example returns `LoyaltyAccountID.LoyaltyID` with an Estonian loyalty brand. It is a loyalty ID, not evidence of a chargeable card token. 2. **Q: How do the synchronous and asynchronous loyalty flows differ, and when is the final payment amount chosen?**
   **A:** In the synchronous flow the POS first sends a card-acquisition request and checks its identifiers against a back-end profile. The required `CardAcquisitionTransaction` object may be empty; its `TotalAmount` can be omitted if unknown or changed later. The POS retains the acquisition response's `POIData.POITransactionID` timestamp and transaction ID, then includes them in `PaymentData.CardAcquisitionReference` on a distinct payment request. A recognized shopper can be offered a benefit before that payment; the payment's `PaymentTransaction.AmountsReq.RequestedAmount` is the final amount after any discount. A new shopper's profile and identifiers require consent. In the asynchronous route the merchant makes a payment, waits for an `AUTHORISATION` webhook, then updates loyalty state from the webhook identifiers; Adyen says that route cannot apply an on-the-spot discount before payment. Card acquisition, response examples, and webhook instructions do not prove an actual authorization, settlement or completed loyalty update.
