# C08 card-based identification — fixed query answers

1. **Can the `alias` or `PaymentToken.TokenValue` in this guide be used to charge a shopper or prove who they are?** The dated page says `alias` represents the **presented card number (PAN)** for later card recognition and explicitly says the alias **cannot be used for payments**. If the optional `TokenRequestedType=Customer` is sent, the same alias also appears in `PaymentInstrumentData.CardData.PaymentToken.TokenValue`; that field placement does not change its purpose. The parking flow matches card presentations to a merchant backend record; it does not verify a person's civil identity, authorize a payment or define PAR/PSP-reference semantics. Evidence: `raw/adyen/docs/point-of-sale/shopper-recognition/identification/2026-09-15.md`, `## Card alias` lines 39–47 and `## Identify the customer` lines 84–150; proposed `[[source-adyen-docs-point-of-sale-shopper-recognition-identification]]`.

2. **In the parking example, which acquisition does the payment reference, and what happens to the arrival acquisition?** The merchant first acquires the card **without an amount**, saves its alias plus arrival data, and cancels that acquisition flow with an `EnableService` request to the **same terminal**. At departure it makes a **second** amount-free acquisition, uses the alias to locate the merchant record, calculates the fee, and makes a separate payment whose `PaymentData.CardAcquisitionReference` uses the **second** response's `POIData.POITransactionID.TimeStamp` and `TransactionID`. The first cancellation is not a financial refund or payment reversal, and the page's request example does not prove the later payment succeeded. Evidence: same raw, `## Identify the customer` lines 49–195, `## Recognize the customer` lines 197–294 and `## Make a payment` lines 296–347; proposed `[[source-adyen-docs-point-of-sale-shopper-recognition-identification]]`.
