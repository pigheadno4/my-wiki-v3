## Overview

This [[adyen]] e315 device-inspection guide was last modified in **2019** and captured 2026-09-15 under terminal-deprecation manuals. It assigns staff visual and behavioral checks for physical tampering and says to stop using the device and contact Support when in doubt. It is a historical checklist for [[adyen-payment-terminals]], not an inspection certificate, current PCI-status determination or assurance that an e315 can process transactions now. The separately captured [[source-adyen-docs-point-of-sale-user-manuals-terminal-deprecation|eVo/Verix deprecation schedule]] lists **29 March 2024** as the e315 “No more transactions” date.

## Key takeaways

- Staff are told to compare the device with manual pictures and check that the Adyen or company logo appears after power-on; they must not use a damaged or sticker-covered device (raw lines 20–26).
- Before giving a purported third-party repair or maintenance person access, staff must verify that person's identity. The guide also calls for regular camera/skimmer checks, verification before installation, replacement or return, awareness of suspicious unplugging or opening, and reporting tampering or substitution to appropriate personnel (raw lines 28–36).
- The guide says staff must not see customers' PINs on a handheld device without a privacy shield (raw line 38). It calls for weekly visual integrity inspections, and says to stop use and contact its Support Team when in doubt (raw line 40).
- The page's general description of secure, certified terminals does not certify this particular unit, prove a completed inspection, override the later e315 deprecation date, or establish live payment authorization or settlement.
