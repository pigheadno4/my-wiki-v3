# Fixed retrieval queries — Endless Aisle ship home

Q1. What is this Adyen page for, what is its scope, and what should it not be used to infer?
Answer: It is a retail use-case and implementation guide for ordering online items with staff in a physical store, authorizing on a terminal, shipping to the shopper, and capturing the ordered items at shipment. It also covers mixed baskets, delivery-address lookup, and store/warehouse returns. It does not establish that shipment itself proves a completed capture, that the merchant's fulfillment or inventory systems are provided by Adyen, or a universal shopper identity/consent rule. Evidence: pinned raw lines 19–30, 37–39, 80–82, 105–115, 121–132, 219–223. Canonical route: https://docs.adyen.com/unified-commerce/retail-use-cases/endless-aisle/e-a-ship-home

Q2. What capabilities, prerequisites, and consequential warnings govern the ship-home and mixed-basket flow?
Answer: Both Adyen ecommerce and POS integrations are needed. The merchant needs a consolidated order/inventory management or ERP system, or alternatively an orchestration layer that communicates with its subsystems and consumes webhooks; store and warehouse inventory must be updateable after returns. Ordered items need manual capture, with multiple partial captures enabled by Support for split shipments; mixed baskets use either one same-merchant-account transaction with partial captures or two transactions with separate in-store capture. Merchant Account Sharing across accounts needs compliance approval and terminals need a local legal entity. Card alias/PAR address lookup needs shopper-identifying responses enabled and profile storage; original PSP reference, full terminal transaction ID and timestamp must be retained for capture and route-specific referenced refunds.
