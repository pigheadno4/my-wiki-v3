# C09 retrieval probes — reserve online, try in store

Q1. In this click-and-collect scenario, what happens when the shopper reserves online, and when is money captured?
Expected: This 2026-09-15 Adyen guide describes a shopper reserving items online, trying them at the pickup store, then deciding what to buy. The ecommerce side makes a Checkout API payment request with manual capture, so the amount is authorized but remains uncaptured at reservation. Capture follows the in-store purchase decision. The merchant needs both ecommerce and POS integrations plus linked order/inventory or webhook-consuming orchestration; POS changes must update online inventory. This does not prove that a stock reservation is a completed payment. Q2. How does the guide handle fewer or extra items and shopper identification, and which limits matter?
Expected: Full purchase uses full Checkout capture; fewer items use partial capture, with the guide saying the remainder is released. For extra items, increasing the original authorization and capturing the total requires pre-authorization and scheme/payment-method eligibility; the alternative captures the reservation and charges extra items separately through Terminal API. Card recognition works for online card or NFC-wallet reservations, not QR wallets, BNPL or other alternative methods, so the guide asks merchants to support profile identification too. Obtain explicit permission before saving shopper details. The merchant saves alias when available, PAR, PSP reference and amount after the `AUTHORISATION` webhook; an NFC-wallet transaction may have no alias and an alias cannot pay. At pickup, Terminal API card acquisition returns identifiers for a merchant-record lookup, then the flow is stopped. Request examples do not establish successful adjustment, capture, payment or current eligibility. Raw lines 73–82, 92–129, 259–271, 273–329, 331–394, 436–494; same canonical route.
