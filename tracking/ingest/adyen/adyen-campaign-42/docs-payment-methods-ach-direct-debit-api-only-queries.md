## Q1 — What is this page for, and what cannot be inferred?

[[source-adyen-docs-payment-methods-ach-direct-debit-api-only]] is the API-only ACH Direct Debit add-on for a merchant-built form, `/paymentMethods` and `/payments` (raw lines 15–46, 80–92). It does not itself validate consumer ownership, establish a signed mandate, prove present merchant eligibility or show final debit success. The related [[source-adyen-docs-payment-methods-ach-direct-debit|ACH overview]] requires a US registered business entity and consumer account validation, says ACH lacks real-time authorization and that chargebacks cannot be defended (overview raw lines 23–29, 35–37, 61–74). The child page's test account formats and `chargeback:R01` simulate behavior only (this raw lines 441–457).

## Q2 — What central capability, prerequisites, and warnings are stated?

The guide requires an existing API-only integration, ACH enabled in Customer Area and the direct-debit notice-of-change webhook; the bank-refund role is specifically for refunds (raw lines 23–31). `/paymentMethods` lists US/PR and USD, while the ACH overview separately requires a registered US business entity (this raw lines 33–40; overview raw lines 23–25). The form gathers number, nine-digit ACH routing number, checking/savings type, holder name and personal/business type to obtain a mandate; missing account type defaults to checking and may cause a savings-account chargeback (this raw lines 42–61). `/payments` accepts clear or encrypted account/routing fields and a required owner name (raw lines 80–92). Nacha requires correct SEC classification: this page says business account type maps to CCD and name inference may fall back to WEB, but its table says CDD and marks the explicit field optional; the overview says API-only must specify the SEC code and a signed mandate is independently required (this raw lines 63–92; overview raw lines 47–63). The examples do not provide encryption generation and include malformed copy-ready curl/JSON, so use API authority for implementation (this raw lines 98–119, 255–293). Refunds require settlement plus the bank-refund role, and reversals/blocked accounts remain possible (overview raw lines 65–128).
