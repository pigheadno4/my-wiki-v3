# C22 worker retrieval checks — docs-issuing-raise-disputes-fraud

## Q1: Who raises a fraud dispute, for what, and what should happen to a lost or stolen card?

The captured Issuing guide addresses the platform acting on an Adyen-issued physical or virtual cardholder's behalf (raw lines 17–21). It says to close the card and submit fraud disputes for fraudulent transactions, and to close a suspected lost or stolen card immediately even without fraudulent transactions (line 21). For a dispute, locate the transaction ID, create a `type: fraud` draft through Transfers API `POST /disputes`, optionally attach evidence, let the cardholder review, then submit if confirmed (lines 27–47, 68–100, 124–215). ## Q2: What limits the dispute outcome or report-only interpretation?

`fraudInfo.reportOnly: true` indicates no further action such as chargeback or fee reversal (raw line 45). Its example mentions a lost/stolen card with no fraudulent transactions, but the POST table still requires `transactionId` (lines 29–47); the page leaves that case unresolved. Creating a dispute yields a draft, not a card-scheme result (lines 68–100). Once submitted, edits, attachment changes and closure are unavailable (lines 209–229); chargeback is conditional on success, and later statuses and transfers require webhook/ARN correlation (lines 211, 255–259). The example POST response for dispute `4VXM2L64EHSACNUU` has ARN `15265674260001677835046` (lines 82, 96), while its created and updated webhook examples show `715265674260001677835046` (lines 108, 117, 237, 246); these examples cannot be treated as one consistent ARN trace, and the page does not say which ARN is authoritative.
