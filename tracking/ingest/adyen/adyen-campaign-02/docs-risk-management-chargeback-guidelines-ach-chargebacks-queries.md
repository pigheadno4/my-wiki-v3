# ACH Direct Debit returns retrieval checks

1. **Purpose, scope, and inference boundary.** This 2026-09-15 Adyen guide calls ACH Direct Debit chargebacks *returns*, says they cannot be defended, and explains risk reduction, notice/webhook stages, refund overlap, reason-code retry decisions, and a test fixture (`raw/adyen/docs/risk-management/chargeback-guidelines/ach-chargebacks/2026-09-15.md:15-23,31-50,101-107,132-147`). ACH has no real-time authorization guarantee: a debit initially reported `Authorised` can return after capture and settlement (`:25-27,553-557`). The stated three-to-five-day typical insufficient-funds timing and one-/two-year dispute windows are dated source claims, not a live determination of a specific return's deadline (`:27`). The guide does not prove a merchant's current return status, Nacha rule applicability, webhook delivery, or whether a particular retry is permitted without its reason code and context.

Adyen delays refunds three days after settlement; a customer return in that period cancels a requested refund and yields `CHARGEBACK` (`raw/adyen/docs/risk-management/chargeback-guidelines/ach-chargebacks/2026-09-15.md:37-39`). An optional Notice of Change can update stored details in many cases, but the merchant should inspect `ach.notificationOfChange` for missing details (`:54-58`). Permission and information stages do not debit funds; Chargeback does (`:83-99`). For retries, inspect the complete table at `:101-130`: R01/R09 allow at most two retries within 30 days of original authorization; R11 allows a retry within 60 days of settlement while requiring recurring-payment suspension and issue resolution; R02/R07 forbid retry (`:111-121`). Repeated impossible retries may incur Nacha fines and an Adyen block on new ACH Direct Debit transactions (`:105`).
