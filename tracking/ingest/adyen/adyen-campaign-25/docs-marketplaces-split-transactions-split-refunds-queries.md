**Q1. How does a Marketplace platform split a refund, and what are the prerequisites?**
Use the original payment PSP reference in `POST /payments/{paymentPspReference}/refunds`, with Checkout API credentials, standard webhook handling, required `amount`/`merchantAccount`, and explicit `splits` to assign deductions across user/platform balance accounts (raw lines 19, 21–49, 55–68). The refund cannot exceed captured value and its top-level currency matches authorization; some methods lack partial refunds, deductions are limited to accounts credited by the payment, and the original split's `pspReference` values must be reused (raw lines 30–33, 45–49). Explicit splits are strongly recommended for partial refunds; outcome comes later in a `REFUND` webhook (raw lines 33, 49, 789). **Q2. What fallback and evidence boundaries matter when refund splits are omitted or targets are invalid?**
A **full** refund without splits reuses the **authorization**, not capture, split ratio; absent authorization splits, the full amount debits the liable account (raw lines 511–521). The page does not define allocation for a partial refund lacking splits. Format-only API validation can leave the full transaction and fees on the liable balance account if a target balance account is nonexistent or tied to a closed holder, even when the request succeeds (raw lines 791–795). Per-split transfer webhooks progress from outgoing/`received` to `authorised` and `refunded`, with a separate booked transaction event; request success or first transfer status is not booking or customer-receipt proof (raw lines 797–811, 832–834, 902–904, 986–989, 1087–1089). The TEST fee transfer ID disagrees with its booked transaction's embedded transfer ID; fee and commission booked transaction account IDs disagree with their transfer examples, although the commission transfer ID itself matches. All three booked transaction samples also reuse one transaction ID, so do not use these illustrative IDs as a join rule (raw lines 1093–1125, 1167–1171, 1205, 1410–1443, 1475–1487, 1522, 1727–1760).
