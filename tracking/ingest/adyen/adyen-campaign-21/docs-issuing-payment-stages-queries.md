Q1. In Issuing, who requests and receives the card payment, and when does the cardholder's balance account actually lose funds?
Answer: The processing merchant sends a card-payment request to Adyen as issuer; the request creates a `balancePlatform.transfer.created` event (pinned raw lines 19, 30–32). Adyen reports authorisation result by transfer-updated webhook, but an authorised amount is reserved, not yet deducted (lines 34–40). At capture, the merchant asks for the reserved funds, and Adyen deducts the captured amount from the cardholder balance account and sends it to the processing merchant (lines 66–73). Q2. Which events can alter an authorisation or cause a debit despite no valid authorisation, and what does a refund webhook prove?
Answer: Merchant adjustment, expiry or cancellation change/release the reserved amount (pinned raw lines 42–56). A scheme's later advice overrides a conflicting initial Adyen decision and determines final status and hold changes (lines 58–64). A forced capture can occur without valid authorisation; Adyen says it must process it, with `syntheticAuthorisation` in the transfer webhook (lines 114–123). It can cause a negative balance or debit the platform's liable balance account when the linked account is inactive/blocked (lines 126–130). A refund transfer-updated event reports the merchant's **refund request**, not confirmed credit back (lines 136–138).
