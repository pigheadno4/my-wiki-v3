## Q2 — Central capability, prerequisites, and warnings

**Query:** How do fee-type splits and precedence work, what is the default, and what evidence and warnings matter when reconciling the fees?

**Expected answer:** A platform already processing payments has no additional stated preparation for this feature. The page lists individual `Interchange`, `SchemeFee`, `AdyenMarkup` and `AdyenCommission`; `AcquiringFees` aggregates the first two, `AdyenFees` the latter two, and `PaymentFee` all transaction fees. A more granular fee split overrides only its share of an aggregate destination. The payment/capture flow uses fee `splits` with account, type and unique reference; description is optional. With no fee booking instructions, Adyen adds `PaymentFee` to the platform liable balance account. Reconcile actual per-split transfer events, booked transactions and Balance Platform Accounting Report aggregate rows/atomic columns; an `Authorised` payment, received/authorised transfer or illustrative test event is not proof of completed capture, booking or payout. The raw source contradicts itself on the AdyenFees example destination (user at line 1141 versus liable at 99–104, 1147 and 1301), the field table's user-account wording versus its liable-account example (line 113), and EUR prose versus USD payment sample. Its sample webhooks also disagree: the AcquiringFees captured transfer changes from user balance account BA...01 to BA...02, the AdyenFees booked transaction names user BA...01 after a liable-account transfer, its transfer ID differs from the preceding transfer, and both booked fee examples reuse a transaction ID. Do not use these sample payloads as a normative join or destination map; verify the actual request/schema and account destination. Do not infer payout, bank receipt, partial-refund or chargeback fee reversal behavior here.
