# Fixed retrieval checks — docs-platforms-multi-pay-in-top-up-multi-pay-in

**Q1 — Purpose, scope, and non-inference.** What does this top-up operation move, why, and what must not be inferred?

**Answer:** It is an internal transfer from the platform's liable balance account to its multi pay-in balance account when a third-party pay-in partner's withholding or bank-cost offset leaves a shortfall against planned user allocation (raw lines 15–20). It is not a bank-funded top-up, user-balance allocation, partner settlement confirmation, seller/merchant-of-record determination, or proof of live funds movement. The source is a 2026-09-15 documentation capture; no current account eligibility, country list, currency list, or transfer limit is established by the USD test example (raw lines 1–12, 46–70). **Q2 — Central capability, prerequisites, and warnings.** How is the top-up requested, and which events distinguish authorisation from booked debit and credit?

**Answer:** The guide requires Transfers API credentials and named Transfer/Transaction webhook subscription (raw lines 21–29). POST `/transfers` with amount currency/value, source liable `balanceAccountId`, destination multi pay-in `counterparty.balanceAccountId`, and `category=internal`; description/reference are optional matching aids (raw lines 31–46). `status=authorised` is the request-level success condition in the guide; `refused`/`notEnoughBalance` is a possible failure example, not a guarantee (raw lines 352–392). On the liable leg, `direction=outgoing`; on multi pay-in, `direction=incoming`, both `internalTransfer`. Follow received → authorised → booked and the corresponding transaction webhooks for each balance account before asserting booked funds (raw lines 394–427, 431–711, 713–1001). The sample response amount `10000` conflicts with the USD 2.40/`240` request; the liable booked transaction's positive `240` conflicts with its debit narrative/mutation; some updated-stage sample `type` fields say created. Use actual IDs, amounts, signs, statuses, currency and timestamps rather than those examples as finality proof (raw lines 46–70, 352–388, 579–709, 791–864). A later separate step allocates third-party payments to users (raw lines 1003–1009).
