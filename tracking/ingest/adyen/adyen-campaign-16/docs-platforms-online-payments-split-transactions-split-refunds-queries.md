## Q1 — What is this page for, where does it apply, and what must it not imply?

This 2026-09-15 Platforms page covers refund debits split among user/platform balance accounts through a Checkout API refund request (raw lines 15–19, 35–49). It is a refund-booking guide, not authorization or capture split handling, chargeback handling, merchant-of-record identification, or proof that a shopper has received funds. The outcome is asynchronous in a `REFUND` webhook (lines 30–33, 789); test examples are illustrative (lines 76–107, 813–1767). ## Q2 — What are the central capability, prerequisites, split/fallback conditions, and warnings?

Use original payment PSP reference, Checkout credentials, `POST /payments/{paymentPspReference}/refunds`, amount at most captured value with authorization currency, merchant account, and webhook handling (raw lines 27–49). Payment-method support constrains partial refunds, and debits are limited to accounts originally credited (30–32). Explicit splits identify balance-account, commission, fee or remainder allocation; Adyen strongly recommends them for partial refunds (49, 53–68). Omitting splits for a full refund reuses the authorization ratio, not capture, and if authorization has no splits debits the whole amount to the liable balance (511–521). Format validation does not validate account IDs; nonexistent accounts or a closed holder route the full transaction and fees to the liable balance, with any corrective internal transfer separate (791–795). Track the refund with `REFUND`, transfer and transaction webhooks and distinct original/modification PSP references (789, 797–811, 813–1767). The table labels `account` required for each split, but the Commission example omits it (59, 88–103); do not resolve that from the example alone. Top-level refund currency and conditional split deduction currency are distinct (45–46, 60). No specific web-service role or live operation is evidenced.
