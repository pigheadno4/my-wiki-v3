# Metronome Campaign 23 Retrospective

## Outcome

- Final content quality: five of five pages approved; the fixed three-page query audit passed 9/9 with no expansion.
- First-pass efficiency: five of five pages approved on attempt 1.
- Retry shape: five worker attempts and five independent full reviews; zero targeted reviews, semantic retries, failed attempts, or rejected jobs.
- Time to final reviewer approval: 981 seconds (16 minutes 21 seconds) from the recorded campaign start.
- Total campaign elapsed time through promotion, close validation, and final state write: 1,603 seconds (26 minutes 43 seconds).
- Coordinator repairs: 6

## What changed

The Minimum Sufficient Source contract asked workers to preserve query-critical facts, material boundaries and contradictions, primary concept routes, a coverage map, and an exact raw deep-dive link without reproducing complete schemas or procedures. Reviewers used the same bounded blocking contract: secondary concept coverage, ordinary raw detail, wording, formatting, and mechanical catalog work did not trigger another complete read.

This produced concise source routers across all five archetypes while keeping the complete immutable raw page as the detailed evidence layer. Independent reviewers found no blocking factual, authority, contradiction, primary-concept, or navigation defect in any first attempt.

## Bounded coordinator work

Six mechanical repair groups changed no factual meaning: use the existing balance-retrieval heading for the contract concept; place the legacy grant invoice block under the existing correction section with a clear sibling heading; add explicit Azure sibling headings so Azure facts do not appear to remain inside AWS subsections; correct the Azure late-event suggestion from quote index 3 to 4 in both receipt views; reconcile the previously stale company body count while updating the new campaign totals; and remove accidental literal patch markers from the first line of newly inserted concept blocks before final validation. Optional reviewer suggestions to add still more quote excerpts were not applied because the exact raw route already satisfied the approved contract.

Reviewer-order persistence recurred as known operational debt: scheduler output changed jobs to `reviewing`, but the attempt `input.json` still contained its worker order until the coordinator materialized the trusted review order. Reviewers failed closed, no raw or candidate was read under the wrong order, and no attempt was duplicated. This is worth one small future coordinator fix, not another registry or monitoring layer.

## Comparison with Campaigns 20 through 22

- Campaign 20: 0/5 first-pass approvals, 12 attempts and 12 reviews, 3 targeted reviews, 3,464 seconds total.
- Campaign 21: 0/5 first-pass approvals, 13 attempts and 13 reviews, 4 targeted reviews, about 44 minutes to final approval and 3,375 seconds through close.
- Campaign 22: 0/5 first-pass approvals, 11 attempts and 11 reviews, 1 targeted review; elapsed time was not valid because of a user-requested pause.
- Campaign 23: 5/5 first-pass approvals, 5 attempts and 5 full reviews, no retry, 981 seconds to final approval, and 1,603 seconds through campaign close.

This is a substantial improvement in this bounded sample, not proof that every provider or document set will behave the same way.

## Defect attribution and lessons

No blocking reviewer defect recurred, so `tracking/ingest/metronome/lessons.md` is unchanged. Non-blocking findings were limited to target anchors, heading placement, quote indexing, and catalog counts. The existing archetype lessons remained useful as short attention guides, but the main speed gain came from changing the acceptance contract rather than adding more checklist detail.

## Recommendation

Go for one separately approved, larger Metronome confirmation campaign using the same Minimum Sufficient Source contract and dynamic native Sol scheduling. Keep complete first review for that confirmation, retain coordinator-only canonical writes, and avoid new registries or performance machinery. If first-pass quality remains high, decide separately whether reviewer sampling or alternative worker models are economically justified. Do not roll this result across Stripe, Adyen, PayPal, or Braintree yet.
