## Summary

This [[stripe]] page records Klarna network eligibility, marketing, termination and merchant duties. The 2026-11-07 capture expands the Klarna-specific prohibited categories and adds a **prospective November 20, 2026** EU consumer-credit marketing disclosure statement. These are dated Stripe/Klarna page claims, not independent legal verification or proof that a particular merchant is eligible. Klarna separately decides whether a customer can use Klarna for a purchase; Stripe acquires the purchase for the merchant and settles merchant funds.

## Prohibited business categories

In addition to Stripe-wide restricted businesses, the **November 2026** Klarna list prohibits:

- Charities; political organizations, parties or initiatives; and business-to-business transactions (spelled **“BTB”** in the new capture, versus **“B2B”** in May). The spelling difference alone does not establish a changed policy.
- Money services, including binary options, cash disbursements, foreign currency/exchange/transfer, digital and crypto currencies, exchanges, tokens and other crypto assets.
- Stored-value cards, vouchers and gift cards.
- Bailiff, bankruptcy, insolvency, fines, claims-management and debt-collection services; court costs, bail and bond payments.
- Gambling, betting and lotteries, including government lotteries; direct marketing such as inbound telemarketing; and fundraising organizations, including crowdfunding platforms.

The **2026-05-06** capture's short list named only charities, political organizations and B2B; it is historical, not a complete November list. The Payment methods settings page is an account eligibility route, not confirmation of approval. Category names and linked terms should be checked for a specific business rather than generalized beyond the captured list.

## Marketing and branding

If a merchant markets Klarna on its website, Stripe says it must follow Klarna marketing guides. Stripe **recommends** the Payment Method Messaging Element on product and cart pages because it automatically handles **Klarna brand compliance**. This does not promise that displaying the Element satisfies every UK or EU legal disclosure or approves a consumer for financing.

For UK Klarna BNPL advertising, both captures say Klarna-approved messaging is required under additional FCA-related requirements and warn that failure can result in criminal charges. The November capture changes the destination of the linked UK rules; that link change alone does not establish a new UK rule.

The **2026-11-07** page additionally says that, **beginning November 20, 2026**, marketing Klarna **credit products to consumers in the EU** triggers additional EU Consumer Credit Directive (CCD2) disclosures. It says to use Klarna pre-approved copy and applicable disclosures and links country guidance for Austria, Belgium, Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Italy, Slovakia and Sweden. The capture does not independently establish current law, each linked country's rule, or that this applies to every Klarna merchant or every payment. The start date is prospective relative to the capture and this update.

## Termination and merchant duties

Klarna has additional suspension/termination rights beyond the Stripe Services Agreement, including breach of prohibited categories or high dispute rates not promptly remedied. By accepting Klarna, merchants acknowledge that Klarna decides customer use and has the right to receive payment from Klarna customers; Stripe acquires those purchases and settles funds to merchants.

Merchants must give customers required or requested instructions/documents (including VAT documents) naming **Klarna as payee** and excluding merchant bank details; not impose Klarna surcharges or higher prices or otherwise act unfairly toward Klarna; promptly follow Stripe directions to stop order processing or shipping for fraud-risk reduction; avoid confusingly similar Klarna trademark designs; and not permit purchases by owners, directors, employees or others reasonably sharing the merchant's financial interest.

## Australian DDO qualification

For an Australian business, the page says **most Stripe users need do nothing** for Klarna's Design and Distribution Obligations (DDO). A business that actively promotes or recommends Klarna Pay in 4 **might** be considered a distributor and **might** have duties to work with Klarna consistent with its Target Market Determination, promptly advise Klarna of significant adverse dealings such as major/multiple harm complaints or an under-18 purchase, and provide complaint or adverse-dealing reports/information **if requested**. Both May and November captures use this conditional language; the former summary's definitive “are distributors” overstates them.
