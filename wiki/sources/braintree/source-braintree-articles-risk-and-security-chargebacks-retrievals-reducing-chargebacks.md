## Overview

This collected [[braintree]] article gives merchants operational guidance intended to reduce chargeback frequency through customer and transaction information, transparent purchase and subscription practices, accessible support and refunds, recognizable statement descriptors, internal monitoring, CAPTCHA and fraud tools. It expressly says chargebacks cannot be prevented entirely. This is general guidance from a 2026-09-16 snapshot, not independent or current bank/card-network rules, merchant-account eligibility, or a guaranteed outcome.

## Key takeaways

- The article recommends collecting customer information with each transaction and identifies billing street address, billing postal code and CVV as particularly important.
- It says terms and conditions should be easy to find and understand, recommends clearly displaying contact information on each website page, and advises prompt delivery with visible shipping timelines so customer expectations are set.
- For a free trial preceding subscription or recurring billing, it says merchants should always provide an option to opt out of the paid service, recommends not collecting payment information for the free trial, and says merchants should always be transparent about when and how much the customer will be charged if continuing afterward.
- It presents an easy, efficient refund process as the most effective way to prevent dissatisfied customers from seeking card-company resolution and says merchants should keep customers informed during the refund process. It also recommends a statement descriptor that clearly identifies the company and includes contact information.
- Suggested internal practices are to review dormant or inactive subscription accounts, repeated customer-service complaints, third-party reviews and repeat chargeback activity. The article says CAPTCHA can help prevent carding attacks and resulting fraudulent-charge chargebacks, and routes readers to Braintree's fraud-tools guide for varying protection levels.
