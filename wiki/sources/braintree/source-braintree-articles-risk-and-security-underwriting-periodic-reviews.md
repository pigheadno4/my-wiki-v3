## Overview

This collected Braintree article describes periodic underwriting reviews performed by Braintree in its stated role as the merchant account provider. The reviews refresh information about a merchant's business model, billing practices, expected processing volumes and financial health so Braintree can reassess current and future processing needs.

## Key takeaways

- The article describes periodic reviews as a standard risk-management process following initial onboarding. It says review cadence can be quarterly, semi-annual or annual based on the business's risk profile, and that the Underwriting team requests the information needed for its assessment.
- Requested material can include recent financial statements, confirmation of the business model and growth expectations for the coming year. The page says merchants may reply with attachments or upload documents securely through the Control Panel.
- Braintree frames the reviews as a way to avoid surprises that might otherwise result in held funds, a required reserve or termination of the processing relationship. This is the article's explanation of its own review process, not a finding that any particular account will receive one of those outcomes.
- The page says that providing requested documentation within the specified time avoids processing or funding delays during review. Its separate important notice asks for documents within two weeks and says Braintree may place a funding hold on the account if significant time passes after review initiation without a response.
- A merchant unable to provide the exact requested documents should tell the Underwriting team; the page says that team can assess whether alternatives provide enough information to complete the review. It also says requested review documents will not be published and points to PayPal privacy policies for handling financial information.

> [!warning] Account and snapshot boundary
> This is captured Braintree guidance for its own merchant-account underwriting process. The stated cadence depends on the business's risk profile, and the timing, document sufficiency, reserve, hold or termination language does not establish a current rule for every account, legal advice, current-law authority, account approval or proof that a review or funding decision occurred.
