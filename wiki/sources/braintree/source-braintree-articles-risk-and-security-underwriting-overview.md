## Overview

This collected Braintree article explains why Braintree says merchant-account-provider loss exposure makes it cautious in underwriting and risk management. It identifies examples of business models and billing methods that it considers higher risk, then describes personal or corporate guarantees and reserves as two conditional ways it may mitigate that exposure.

## Key takeaways

- Braintree says merchant account providers like it are financially liable for merchant losses and illustrates that exposure with a merchant that stops delivering a prepaid annual service. The example explains the page's underwriting rationale; it is not a universal legal rule or proof of liability in a specific case.
- The page identifies travel and several other industries as higher-risk examples, and annual billing, retainers or account credit, and aggregation as billing methods that increase risk. It says high-risk businesses must demonstrate financial strength and that some industries may be refused by many merchant account providers; use the raw lists for the captured examples, not as a current eligibility or prohibited-business catalog.
- Braintree says it can sometimes reduce its exposure enough to process for a high-risk business. After reviewing company financials, it may require a personal or corporate guarantee; this is conditional guidance, not evidence of approval or of a requirement for every merchant.
- If Braintree determines during the application process that a reserve is necessary, the page says it may collect the reserve before processing begins or withhold a percentage of transaction revenue until the reserve limit is reached. The snapshot does not provide a fixed percentage, amount, duration, or approval criterion.

> [!warning] Suspected integration compromise
> A merchant that believes its Braintree integration may have been compromised is directed to contact Braintree for assistance. This captured escalation route is not evidence that an incident was investigated or resolved.

> [!warning] Insurance and indemnity statement
> The collected page states that Braintree does not have cyberinsurance and does not indemnify, and describes non-indemnification as the gateway-provider industry standard. Treat this as the page's 2026-09-16 statement, not current law, compliance certification, or proof of the terms governing a particular merchant.
