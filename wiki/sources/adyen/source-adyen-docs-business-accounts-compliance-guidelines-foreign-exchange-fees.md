## Overview

This 2026-09-15-captured Adyen financial-products and Adyen for Platforms compliance-guidelines page explains how to present FX rates, conversion values and additional fees when a user's transaction converts currencies. Although filed under Business Accounts, it says its UK Financial Conduct Authority-based presentation guidance applies to platforms in all regions. The page expressly disclaims legal advice; its sample figures are examples, not current rates, quotes or proof of a completed international payment. [[adyen-currencies-and-fx]]

## Key takeaways

- For a transaction with any currency conversion, the page says users must see the FX rate and additional fees at the transaction point and in statements covering the relevant period. The examples aim to make the conversion rate, conversion value and paid fee legible. See raw lines 19-23 and 68-72.
- A variable-fee example presents £100 paid, a 3%/£3 fee, £97 converted at 1 GBP to 1.30 USD, and $126.10 received. Additional fixed-fee examples show different ways of displaying a £5 fee and a 3% markup sentence. These are illustrative figures, not a universal Adyen tariff or merchant rate. See raw lines 25-36 and 50-72.
- The first fixed-fee table is internally inconsistent: it labels a £0 fixed fee but £3 total fees added, and labels the recipient amount £126.10 despite a displayed 1.26 USD-per-GBP rate. Do not reuse that table as a calculation or price promise. See raw lines 38-48.
- The third-party-fee examples distinguish an upfront `Pay all fees?` option, described as covering intermediary deductions and guaranteeing the intended recipient amount, from a case where the recipient bears third-party fees deducted from the amount sent. Keep that guarantee attached to the illustrated option; the raw does not establish the availability or outcome of any real transfer. See raw lines 74-99.
