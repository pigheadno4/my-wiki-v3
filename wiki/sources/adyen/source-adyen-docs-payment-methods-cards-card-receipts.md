## Overview

Adyen’s ecommerce card-receipt guidelines for online card sales and online-card refunds. The page says shoppers must receive a receipt and lists delivery-format, access, content and prohibited-data rules. It was last modified 2022-01-19 and captured 2026-09-15; this is a provider documentation snapshot, not proof of current legal compliance, receipt delivery, capture/refund completion or a successful payment.

## Key takeaways

- For an online card payment or refund, the guide says to provide a shopper receipt. If delivered by email, use a static format that cannot be easily manipulated, put the business name in the subject or first line, and state that the email contains the receipt or a link. If providing a website/app link, give clear access instructions, link directly to the receipt, and keep it available at least 120 calendar days **after the capture date** (raw lines 19–31). The page does not specify a separate clock for a refund-only receipt.
- The listed receipt fields are recognizable business name, shopper-local transaction date (time optional), itemized goods/services description, sale/refund type, full captured/refunded amount, currency symbol, authorization code, last four card digits, scheme brand, registered business address, website URL, customer-service contact, and cancellation/return/refund policies or a direct link to them (raw lines 33–49). The authorization-code field is a listed content item, not evidence that authorization or capture occurred.
- Prohibitions are more than four card-number digits, any PIN portion or representation, CVC/CVV, and promotional language advertising a competitor card scheme (raw lines 51–67). This page addresses ecommerce card receipts; it does not define POS receipt behavior or demonstrate sending an email or generating a compliant receipt.
